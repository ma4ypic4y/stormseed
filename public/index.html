From a flash
to a decision.

Real weather goes in. A small decision comes out.

  1. Catch a little lightning.

    NOAA’s GOES satellites observe flashes over the Americas and nearby oceans. We fetch a small public snapshot roughly every 30 minutes.

  2. Mix in something private.

    We hash the observations with 32 fresh random bytes from your browser. The lightning is a real input; your browser keeps each new pick unpredictable.

  3. Give every option a fair chance.

    Rejection sampling avoids the bias that a simple remainder can introduce. Copy the answer, or keep a little piece of the storm.

How random is it, really?

Useful for everyday choices and games. It is a hybrid generator, not a certified lightning-only source. Storms cluster, satellite measurements have biases, and a public feed is no longer secret. A hash mixes inputs; it does not create entropy.

We have not measured a defensible number of entropy bits in the lightning feed. The unpredictability of each pick relies on your browser’s cryptographic random generator and SHA-256. Don’t use this site for passwords, money, or audited prize draws.

Is this live? Does it cover the whole planet?

It is a recent snapshot, not a live stream. The observed age is always shown. GitHub’s scheduled refreshes may be delayed. If updates stop, we keep the last real snapshot and label it as older data.

GOES-19 and GOES-18 cover their views of the Western Hemisphere, not all of Earth. GLM detects optical flashes, including lightning inside clouds. These are not all confirmed ground strikes. Observations from the two satellites can overlap.

Can I inspect my result?

Yes. Download the receipt below to see the snapshot fingerprint, source file hashes, browser nonce, range and exact algorithm. With those inputs you can reproduce the number. A receipt does not prove that someone didn’t reroll.

Generate a result first. A receipt for “Choose” includes your list.

Has anyone done this before?

Yes. RANDOM.ORG uses atmospheric radio noise originating from lightning. Researchers have also used lightning radio signals to check the integrity of power-grid measurements.

Stormseed takes a different route: open satellite observations, browser randomness and a useful little interface. Read the research and design rationale ↗
